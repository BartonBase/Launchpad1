# 00: Pre-implementation threat model: NFT launchpad (MPL-Hybrid + Token-2022 transfer tax + VRF lottery)

- **Author:** auditor-a
- **Date:** 2026-09-24
- **Status:** Pre-implementation. `/workspace/launchpad/programs` is empty, so this document reviews the planned design, not code. The on-chain engineer should build against it, and the later code audit will check the code against it.
- **Project facts used here:** Only what `docs/BRIEF.md` says. Where the design is unknown, I ask a question instead of assuming. Any facts about third-party programs (MPL-Hybrid, Token-2022, Anchor, Switchboard, ORAO, Jito) are cited inline. Pinned sources are listed at the end.
- **Scope:** (1) the MPL-Hybrid token⇄NFT swap, (2) Token-2022 TransferFee harvesting, withdrawal, and the fee-funded NFT buy flow, (3) the holder lottery with tickets, VRF, and the prize vault, (4) admin, upgrade, and authority custody, and (5) Anchor account-validation issues that cut across all of them. Out of scope: the web app, the bonding curve or AMM graduation (if it gets built, it needs its own model), and off-chain infrastructure beyond key custody.

Severity key, used in every table: **Critical** means an unprivileged attacker can drain a vault or take an authority, or the whole product is broken by design. **High** means loss of funds or prizes under realistic conditions, or a lottery an attacker can reliably bias. **Medium** means economic leakage, a meaningful DoS, or a loss that needs an unusual precondition. **Low** means a minor bias, griefing, or a hygiene issue.

## 1. The five things most likely to hurt this project

1. **MPL-Hybrid does not support Token-2022 today, so it cannot swap the taxed token.** At commit `aacf1a5` (2026-05-27), every swap instruction takes `token_program: Program<'info, Token>` and `token: Account<'info, Mint>` from `anchor_spl::token` (legacy SPL Token). `validate_token_account` rejects any token account not owned by `spl_token::ID`, and tokens move with `token::transfer`, not `transfer_checked` ([capture.rs](https://github.com/metaplex-foundation/mpl-hybrid/blob/aacf1a53c8a43bf395db7b562c02cf016ce604c5/programs/mpl-hybrid/src/instructions/capture.rs), [utils.rs](https://github.com/metaplex-foundation/mpl-hybrid/blob/aacf1a53c8a43bf395db7b562c02cf016ce604c5/programs/mpl-hybrid/src/utils.rs)). Metaplex's FAQ confirms: "Currently the MPL-Hybrid program only supports Metaplex Core NFT assets and SPL Token fungible assets" ([MPL-Hybrid FAQ](https://www.metaplex.com/docs/smart-contracts/mpl-hybrid/faq)). Token-2022 separately rejects a plain `Transfer` from an account that carries the `TransferFeeAmount` extension (`MintRequiredForTransfer`, [token-2022 processor.rs](https://github.com/solana-program/token-2022/blob/8867f75/program/src/processor.rs)). If the hybrid token and the taxed token are meant to be the same mint, the team has to write or fork a Token-2022-aware swap program, and that program becomes the highest-risk code in the project.
2. **Whoever controls the Core collection's update authority controls the MPL-Hybrid escrow.** `update_escrow_v1` and `update_recipe_v1` accept any signer equal to the collection's `update_authority`. That signer can immediately change the swap `amount`, fees, `fee_location`, and even the `token` mint. There is no timelock, and the handlers carry the comment "Need to add account checks for security" ([update_recipe.rs](https://github.com/metaplex-foundation/mpl-hybrid/blob/aacf1a53c8a43bf395db7b562c02cf016ce604c5/programs/mpl-hybrid/src/instructions/update_recipe.rs)). With one compromised key, an attacker can set `amount` to the escrow balance and release a single NFT to take every token, or set `amount = 0` and capture every NFT.
3. **The fee pot belongs to `withdraw_withheld_authority`.** That authority can withdraw all harvested fees, and `transfer_fee_config_authority` can raise the fee as high as 10,000 bps (100%) two epochs after setting it ([Solana transfer-fee docs](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/transfer-fees), [transfer_fee/mod.rs](https://github.com/solana-program/token-2022/blob/8867f75/interface/src/extension/transfer_fee/mod.rs)). If either is a hot EOA, one leaked key drains or wrecks the product.
4. **Tickets computed from `balance` can be gamed with flash-held tokens.** A Jito bundle runs up to 5 transactions sequentially and atomically in one slot ([Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)), so a bot can buy, register or snapshot, and sell in one bundle. Tokens can also be counted twice by moving them between wallets between registrations. The brief's `floor(balance / threshold)` formula stops wallet-splitting from gaining tickets, but it does nothing about flash-holding or double-counting ([BRIEF.md](../../docs/BRIEF.md)).
5. **Most VRF bugs are integration bugs, not provider bugs.** Common ones: accepting a randomness account the attacker created (Switchboard's `RandomnessAccountData::parse` only checks the discriminator and takes raw bytes, so it cannot check the owner, per [randomness.rs](https://github.com/switchboard-xyz/solana-sdk/blob/2121341/src/on_demand/accounts/randomness.rs)); accepting an already-revealed or stale value; and letting the party who reveals simulate the result and decline to submit (the "selective revelation" attack Switchboard warns about in its [tutorial](https://docs.switchboard.xyz/docs-by-chain/solana-svm/randomness/randomness-tutorial)). The fee-funded NFT buy path, the distribution layer the brief says needs the heaviest scrutiny, has a similar class of bug: arbitrary CPI and caller-chosen prices.

## 2. Assumed components (to be confirmed)

The brief sets these requirements: fixed 1B supply with mint authority revoked at launch; hybrid swap via MPL-Hybrid "at a fixed ratio", unwrapping "back into the exact tokens", with ratios that divide 1B; a Token-2022 transfer tax that "buys NFTs from collections and gives them to holders through a lottery"; tickets = `floor(balance / threshold)`; a minimum holding period; Switchboard or ORAO VRF; and testnets only until a third-party audit ([BRIEF.md](../../docs/BRIEF.md)). A plausible program layout, stated as an **assumption** for threat-modeling only:

| Component | Assumed role | Holds value? |
|---|---|---|
| `launch_config` PDA (per launch) | mint, collection, ratio, threshold, authorities, status | No, but it controls everything |
| MPL-Hybrid escrow PDA (`["escrow", collection]` v1 or `["escrow", authority]` v2), or a custom swap escrow | holds NFTs and fungible tokens that back each other | **Yes** |
| Fee vault (token account owned by a program PDA) | receives withdrawn withheld fees | **Yes** |
| Buy executor | turns fees into NFTs (DEX swap plus marketplace buy, or MPL-Hybrid capture) | Moves value |
| Prize vault PDA | holds prize NFTs (and maybe tokens) until claimed | **Yes** |
| Ticket / stake records, round PDA, randomness binding | lottery state | Decides who gets value |
| Upgrade authority, admin, keeper roles | operations | Can move everything |

|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Fungible tokens in the hybrid escrow | Escrow token account (owner = escrow PDA) | Drained through a swap bug, a config change, or a mint/extension authority (PermanentDelegate) |
| A2 | NFTs in the hybrid escrow | Core assets owned by escrow PDA | Captured for free, rare ones sniped, or taken through a changed config |
| A3 | Withheld transfer fees | Spread across every holder's token account, then on the mint, then in the fee vault | Withdrawn by whoever holds `withdraw_withheld_authority`; stuck if harvest or withdraw breaks |
| A4 | Fee vault balance and SOL from swapping it | Fee vault PDA | Spent on overpriced or fake NFTs, sandwiched on the swap, or drained through arbitrary CPI |
| A5 | Prize vault (NFTs, maybe tokens) | Prize vault PDA | Paid to the wrong winner, claimed twice, or stuck |
| A6 | Lottery integrity | Round, ticket, and randomness state | Biased draws, bought tickets, sybil or flash tickets |
| A7 | Program upgrade authority | BPF upgradeable loader ProgramData | Swaps the program logic, so it controls every PDA |
| A8 | Mint authorities: mint, freeze, `transfer_fee_config_authority`, `withdraw_withheld_authority`, any PermanentDelegate, TransferHook, MintClose, Pausable, or metadata authority | Token-2022 mint and its extensions | Unlimited minting, fee rug, seizure, freezing, pausing |
| A9 | Core collection update authority (it is also the MPL-Hybrid escrow/recipe authority) | MPL Core collection | Reconfigures the escrow (see §1 item 2); rewrites NFT metadata |
| A10 | Admin/config authority of our own programs | `launch_config` | Changes parameters, ends rounds early, redirects funds |

## 4. Trust boundaries

1. **User transactions → our programs.** Every account, amount, and instruction ordering is attacker-controlled, including `remaining_accounts` and the other instructions in the same transaction or bundle.
2. **Our programs → external programs via CPI:** Token-2022, SPL Token, ATA, MPL Core, MPL-Hybrid, Switchboard or ORAO, DEX, and marketplace. We trust them only after pinning their program IDs, and we trust their accounts only after checking owner and discriminator.
3. **Mint and collection authorities → everything.** Token-2022 mint extensions and the Core collection update authority can override our program logic from outside (seize, freeze, pause, change fees, reconfigure the escrow). The code cannot defend against these. Only authority custody and extension choices can.
4. **Off-chain operators and keepers → our programs.** Crankers for harvest, buy, and draw, plus any backend co-signer. They are semi-trusted at most. The design should limit what a compromised keeper can do to "delay", never "steal" or "bias".
5. **VRF oracle → lottery.** Switchboard relies on TEEs and oracle staking ([Switchboard randomness docs](https://docs.switchboard.xyz/docs-by-chain/solana-svm/randomness)). ORAO fulfils requests asynchronously ([ORAO README](https://github.com/orao-network/solana-vrf)). Either way, liveness and honesty are trusted, and our integration must not add weaknesses of its own.
6. **Upgrade authority → program bytes.** Whoever can upgrade can do anything the program can do.

## 5. Attacker model

| Attacker | Capabilities assumed | Main targets |
|---|---|---|
| **Well-funded bots** | Can build any transaction, simulate against current state, run many wallets, and borrow or buy large positions briefly. Pays fees and transfer tax without caring. | Ticket snapshot, rare-NFT sniping, buy-flow front-running, rounding and fee-accounting bugs |
| **MEV searchers / Jito bundles** | Up to 5 transactions executed sequentially and atomically in one slot. The whole bundle fails if any transaction fails ([Jito](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)). That enables "revert unless I win" patterns, sandwiches around our DEX swaps, and buy-snapshot-sell atomicity. | Any randomness readable in-transaction, any price-dependent instruction, any snapshot |
| **Sybils** | Unlimited wallets at the cost of rent. | Per-wallet ticket floors, per-wallet caps, airdrop-like distributions |
| **Malicious accounts / mints passed in** | Fake mints with hostile extensions (PermanentDelegate, TransferHook, frozen DefaultAccountState, MintCloseAuthority), token accounts for the wrong mint or owner, accounts owned by the wrong token program, look-alike Switchboard/ORAO accounts, fake Core assets or collections, fake marketplace programs, duplicate accounts. | Every `UncheckedAccount`, every `InterfaceAccount`, every `remaining_accounts` entry |
| **Compromised admin, keeper, or authority key** | Full control of that key: phishing, leaked `.json` keypair, a malicious insider. | Upgrade authority, collection update authority, fee authorities, config admin, keeper hot wallet |
| **Validator/leader** | Controls transaction ordering within its slots and has limited influence on timestamps. The Switchboard tutorial notes block hashes are predictable and timestamps can be manipulated ([tutorial](https://docs.switchboard.xyz/docs-by-chain/solana-svm/randomness/randomness-tutorial)). | Any use of SlotHashes, Clock, or recent blockhash as randomness |

## 6. Threats and required controls by subsystem

### 6.1 MPL-Hybrid swaps (token ⇄ NFT)

**What MPL-Hybrid does today** (source at commit `aacf1a5`, program ID `MPL4o4wMzndgh8T1NVDxELQCj5UQfYTYEkabX3wNKtb`, Anchor `~0.29`, per [lib.rs](https://github.com/metaplex-foundation/mpl-hybrid/blob/aacf1a53c8a43bf395db7b562c02cf016ce604c5/programs/mpl-hybrid/src/lib.rs) and [Cargo.toml](https://github.com/metaplex-foundation/mpl-hybrid/blob/aacf1a53c8a43bf395db7b562c02cf016ce604c5/programs/mpl-hybrid/Cargo.toml)):
- Instructions: `init_escrow_v1/v2`, `init_recipe_v1`, `init_nft_data_v1`, `capture_v1/v2` (**tokens → NFT**: the user pays `amount` plus a token fee plus SOL fees and receives an NFT from escrow), `release_v1/v2` (**NFT → tokens**: the user gives an NFT and receives `amount` tokens), `update_escrow_v1`, `update_recipe_v1`, `update_new_data_v1`, `migrate_nft_v1`, `migrate_tokens_v1` ([Metaplex swap docs](https://www.metaplex.com/docs/smart-contracts/mpl-hybrid/swapping-tokens-to-nfts)).
- PDAs: EscrowV1 = `["escrow", collection]`; RecipeV1 = `["recipe", collection]`; EscrowV2 = `["escrow", authority]`, so one v2 escrow per authority can back several recipes ([capture_v2.rs](https://github.com/metaplex-foundation/mpl-hybrid/blob/aacf1a53c8a43bf395db7b562c02cf016ce604c5/programs/mpl-hybrid/src/instructions/capture_v2.rs), [init_escrow_v2.rs](https://github.com/metaplex-foundation/mpl-hybrid/blob/aacf1a53c8a43bf395db7b562c02cf016ce604c5/programs/mpl-hybrid/src/instructions/init_escrow_v2.rs)).
- Authority model: to init or update, the signer must equal the Core collection's `update_authority`. The docs say "the authority of the escrow needs to match the authority of the collection to perform metadata updates" ([escrow docs](https://developers.metaplex.com/smart-contracts/mpl-hybrid/escrow)). During swaps, the metadata reroll needs either the escrow/recipe authority as a **co-signer** or the escrow/recipe PDA as the asset's update delegate ([capture.rs](https://github.com/metaplex-foundation/mpl-hybrid/blob/aacf1a53c8a43bf395db7b562c02cf016ce604c5/programs/mpl-hybrid/src/instructions/capture.rs)).
- Reroll randomness: `u64` from SlotHashes bytes `[12..20]`, `saturating_sub(unix_timestamp)`, `wrapping_mul(count)`, `% (max - min)`. All of that is public or predictable within the slot.
- The user picks which `asset` to capture. The only check is that its `update_authority == Collection(escrow.collection)`.
- `path` bits: `NoRerollMetadata`, `BlockCapture`, `BlockRelease`, `BurnOnCapture`, `BurnOnRelease` ([path.rs](https://github.com/metaplex-foundation/mpl-hybrid/blob/aacf1a53c8a43bf395db7b562c02cf016ce604c5/programs/mpl-hybrid/src/state/path.rs)). Only the reroll bit is locked after the first swap.
- A fixed protocol fee wallet receives a rent-derived SOL fee on each swap ([constants.rs](https://github.com/metaplex-foundation/mpl-hybrid/blob/aacf1a53c8a43bf395db7b562c02cf016ce604c5/programs/mpl-hybrid/src/constants.rs)). The FAQ puts it at about 0.005 SOL.
- `capture_v1` and `migrate_nft_v1` store the NFT-transfer CPI result in `let _transfer_nft_result` and never check it. Per the [`solana_cpi::invoke` docs](https://docs.rs/solana-cpi/latest/solana_cpi/fn.invoke.html), an error returned by the callee aborts the transaction anyway, so this is a code smell rather than an exploit. Our own code must never do this.
- Open GitHub issues are operational (insufficient funds, InvalidAccountData on ReleaseV1, escrow not showing NFTs, a stack-frame access violation) ([issues](https://github.com/metaplex-foundation/mpl-hybrid/issues?q=is%3Aissue)). I found no published security advisory. That does not mean none exists; confirm with Metaplex (`security@metaplex.foundation`, per the program's `security_txt`).

| ID | Threat | How an attacker does it | Severity if present | Required design control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-1 | **Taxed Token-2022 mint used with stock MPL-Hybrid** | Not an attack: swaps fail outright (owner check, `Program<Token>`, `MintRequiredForTransfer`). The danger is a rushed fork that half-adds Token-2022. | Critical (design blocker) | Decide Q1 (§8). Either (a) the hybrid token is a separate legacy SPL mint and MPL-Hybrid is used as-is, or (b) write or fork a swap program that uses `Interface<'info, TokenInterface>` pinned to Token-2022 (`address = spl_token_2022::ID`), `InterfaceAccount<Mint>` with `mint::token_program = token_program`, and `transfer_checked` / `transfer_checked_with_fee` only. Option (b) gets the most audit time. |
| H-2 | **Escrow or recipe reconfigured by the collection authority** | Compromised collection update authority calls `update_recipe_v1`: sets `amount` to the full escrow balance and releases one NFT, sets `amount = 0` and captures every NFT, swaps `token` to a worthless mint, or redirects `fee_location`. No timelock. | Critical | Hold the Core collection update authority in a multisig (e.g. Squads) with a timelock, never on a server. If using our own swap program, make ratio, mint, and collection **immutable after launch** (no update instruction) and require `TOTAL_SUPPLY_BASE_UNITS % ratio == 0` at init, per the brief. Monitor and alert on any `update_*` transaction. |
| H-3 | **Hot co-signer key for rerolls** | If the reroll path is enabled and the escrow authority must co-sign every capture, a backend holds the collection authority key online, which turns H-2 into a server compromise. | Critical | Don't co-sign with the collection authority. Either disable reroll (`NoRerollMetadata`) or make the escrow/recipe PDA the asset/collection update delegate so the program signs itself. |
| H-4 | **Predictable reroll traits** | A bot simulates `capture` in the current slot (SlotHashes, timestamp, and `count` are all known) and bundles `capture` with an assertion instruction that reverts unless the rolled URI is rare. Losing attempts cost nothing inside a Jito bundle. | High (if traits differ in value), Low (if cosmetic only) | Don't use MPL-Hybrid's built-in reroll for anything with value. If traits matter, assign them in a separate VRF-driven step (commit in the capture tx, reveal later), or pre-assign metadata and disable reroll. |
| H-5 | **User-chosen asset: rare sniping and adverse selection** | `capture` lets the caller name any escrowed NFT, so bots always take the rarest. `release` accepts any collection NFT for the same fixed `amount`, so the escrow fills with the least valuable NFTs. If fee-funded purchases (6.2) put bought NFTs into this escrow, bots can extract that value immediately. | High (economic) | If escrowed NFTs are not fungible in value, have the program choose the outgoing asset (FIFO queue, or VRF index over an on-chain list). Keep fee-bought prize NFTs in the **prize vault**, never in the hybrid escrow. |
| H-6 | **Fee-unaware amounts in a Token-2022 swap (custom program)** | User transfers `amount` in and the escrow receives `amount - fee`, yet the program credits `amount`. Over time the escrow becomes under-collateralised and the last people to release can't redeem. Or the program pays out `amount`, the user gets less, and the brief's "exact tokens back" promise is broken. | High | Compute the fee with the mint's current-epoch `TransferFeeConfig` (`calculate_epoch_fee` / `calculate_inverse_epoch_fee`) and use `transfer_checked_with_fee`, which fails with `FeeMismatch` if the fee is wrong ([processor.rs](https://github.com/solana-program/token-2022/blob/8867f75/program/src/processor.rs)). Or measure the balance change after `reload()`. Check the backing invariant after every swap. Resolve Q7 (TransferFeeConfig has no per-account exemption field, per [mod.rs](https://github.com/solana-program/token-2022/blob/8867f75/interface/src/extension/transfer_fee/mod.rs)). |
| H-7 | **Fake asset or collection accepted** | Pass a Core asset from another collection, an account not owned by MPL Core, or a spoofed collection account. | High | Check that the asset's owner program is the MPL Core ID, deserialize it with the Core crate, check `update_authority == Collection(config.collection)`, check the asset's current owner is the escrow PDA (capture) or the signer (release), and pin `mpl_core` with `address = MPL_CORE_ID`. |
| H-8 | **Wrong token account / owner / mint on swap** | Pass an escrow token account owned by the attacker, a user token account for a different mint, or an account owned by legacy SPL Token. | High | `associated_token::mint = mint`, `associated_token::authority = escrow`, and `associated_token::token_program = token_program` on the escrow ATA. For the user account, `token::mint` + `token::authority = user` + `token::token_program`. No `UncheckedAccount` for token accounts. |
| H-9 | **Escrow drained through the fungible mint's own authorities** | A PermanentDelegate on the mint transfers straight out of the escrow. A freeze authority freezes the escrow ATA. Pausable halts all transfers. A mint authority mints unlimited tokens and captures every NFT. | Critical (if any exist and are compromised) | Mint allowlist (see T-8). Mint authority `None` at launch (brief requirement). No PermanentDelegate, no Pausable, and freeze authority `None` unless justified. |
| H-10 | **Escrow underfunded at launch / broken backing invariant** | Escrow starts with fewer tokens than `NFTs outstanding × ratio`, so the last people to release can't redeem. | Medium | Fund the escrow in the same transaction or instruction that creates it. Store the expected backing and assert it on every swap. Show the backing live on the site. |
| H-11 | **Swap DoS through a frozen escrow ATA or DefaultAccountState=Frozen** | Mint default state freezes the newly created escrow ATA, so every swap fails ([Neodyme](https://neodyme.io/en/blog/token-2022)). | Medium | Reject mints with DefaultAccountState or a freeze authority, or thaw during setup. Check `!is_frozen()` at init. |

### 6.2 Token-2022 fee harvesting and the fee-funded buy flow

**Relevant semantics** (from the [Solana transfer-fee docs](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/transfer-fees) and [token-2022 source](https://github.com/solana-program/token-2022/tree/8867f75)):
- The fee is withheld **on the destination token account**, and the recipient gets `amount - fee`. `TransferChecked` also withholds. `TransferCheckedWithFee` makes the caller state the expected fee and fails on mismatch.
- `HarvestWithheldTokensToMint` is **permissionless**. It moves withheld amounts from token accounts to the mint. It **logs and skips** accounts that fail (wrong mint, missing extension) instead of reverting, and it does not check freeze state ([transfer_fee/processor.rs](https://github.com/solana-program/token-2022/blob/8867f75/program/src/extension/transfer_fee/processor.rs)).
- `WithdrawWithheldTokensFromMint` and `WithdrawWithheldTokensFromAccounts` need a `withdraw_withheld_authority` signature, and the destination must not be frozen.
- `SetTransferFee` writes the *newer* fee with `epoch = current + 2`. `get_epoch_fee` uses the newer fee once `epoch >= newer.epoch`. `MAX_FEE_BASIS_POINTS = 10_000`, and `maximum_fee` is a `u64` cap per transfer.
- A token account can't be closed while `withheld_amount != 0` (`AccountHasWithheldTransferFees`).
- A plain `Transfer` from an account carrying `TransferFeeAmount` fails with `MintRequiredForTransfer`. Neodyme shows the reverse: token accounts created *before* a mint was closed and reinitialised with a fee have no fee extension, so they can move tokens fee-free with `transfer` ([Neodyme](https://neodyme.io/en/blog/token-2022)).
- Anchor exposes `transfer_checked_with_fee`, `harvest_withheld_tokens_to_mint`, `withdraw_withheld_tokens_from_mint`, `withdraw_withheld_tokens_from_accounts`, and `transfer_fee_set` ([anchor-spl docs](https://docs.rs/anchor-spl/latest/anchor_spl/token_2022_extensions/transfer_fee/index.html)).

| ID | Threat | How an attacker does it | Severity if present | Required design control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T-1 | **Fee rug by an EOA `withdraw_withheld_authority`** | Holder of the key (or whoever steals it) calls `WithdrawWithheldTokensFromMint` into their own account. Nothing on-chain stops it. | Critical | Set `withdraw_withheld_authority` to a **program PDA** (e.g. `["fee_authority", mint]`) so fees can only move through program logic into the fee vault PDA. At launch, the program reads the mint's `TransferFeeConfig` and refuses to activate unless the authority equals that PDA. |
| T-2 | **Fee raised to punitive levels** | `transfer_fee_config_authority` calls `SetTransferFee` with up to 10,000 bps and a huge `maximum_fee`. It takes effect two epochs later. Holders are trapped, or the lottery and buy flow math breaks. | High | Make `transfer_fee_config_authority` `None` at launch if the tax is meant to be fixed, or a multisig with a public policy. Our program must read the **current epoch** fee every time and never cache it. Show the pending "newer" fee in the UI. |
| T-3 | **Transfer-fee bypass through a closed and reinitialised mint** | Neodyme's attack: create the mint without a fee, pre-create token accounts, close the mint (MintCloseAuthority, supply 0), reinit with a fee, then use the old accounts with `transfer` to dodge the fee. | High | Create the mint **inside our launch instruction** (create account, init extensions, init mint, mint 1B, revoke mint authority, all atomic). Forbid MintCloseAuthority. Record the mint in config at creation, so no externally supplied mint is ever accepted. |
| T-4 | **Hostile mint passed to a generic instruction** | If any instruction takes a mint as input (multi-launch factory), the attacker supplies one with PermanentDelegate, a TransferHook to their own program, NonTransferable, ConfidentialTransfer, or a frozen default, to break accounting or DoS vaults. | High | Every instruction takes the mint via `address = launch_config.mint`. Mints come only from our launch instruction. Enforce an extension allowlist at launch (T-8). |
| T-5 | **Harvest accounting assumes success** | Program sums "harvested" amounts from the list it passed, but harvest silently skipped bad accounts, so the program credits more than it got. Or an attacker pads the list to waste compute. | Medium | Measure the mint's `withheld_amount` before and after (and the vault balance before and after `withdraw`, using `reload()`). Credit only the difference. Cap list length per crank. Keep harvest permissionless; it is safe because it can only move fees into the mint. |
| T-6 | **Fee vault drained through the buy executor (arbitrary CPI)** | A "buy NFT" instruction takes the marketplace program and accounts from `remaining_accounts`. The attacker passes their own program, which the vault PDA then signs for, and it transfers the vault out. | Critical | **No arbitrary CPI.** Pin every external program ID (`Program<'info, T>` or `address =`). Only sign PDA seeds for CPIs whose program ID is pinned. Validate each `remaining_accounts` entry (owner, key, derivation). After the CPI, check exact expected balance changes on the vault. |
| T-7 | **Overpaying for NFTs: price chosen by the caller or keeper** | Keeper or bot lists an NFT from an allowed collection at an inflated price, then triggers "buy floor" pointing at its own listing. Or it buys a fake NFT from a look-alike collection. | Critical (with no price bound), High (with loose bounds) | Make the program verify on-chain that the bought asset belongs to an allowlisted collection (Core `Collection(x)` or a verified Token Metadata collection). Enforce a per-NFT price cap and a per-epoch spend cap. Make the keeper a distinct role that can't change caps. If floor price can't be verified on-chain, restrict purchases to multisig-approved transactions (see Q5). Post-condition: the NFT is owned by the prize vault PDA. |
| T-8 | **Our own mint carries dangerous extensions** | PermanentDelegate (can seize the escrow, fee vault, and prize vault), TransferHook (arbitrary code on every transfer, plus extra accounts), MintCloseAuthority (T-3), DefaultAccountState=Frozen, NonTransferable, ConfidentialTransfer (balances hidden, so fee and ticket math breaks), Pausable (halts every transfer). | Critical (PermanentDelegate), High (others) | At launch, parse `PodStateWithExtensions::<PodMint>` and require `get_extension_types() ⊆ {TransferFeeConfig, MetadataPointer, TokenMetadata}` (adjust to the final choice, e.g. group pointers, which Neodyme calls cosmetic). Also require `mint_authority == None` after minting 1B, `freeze_authority == None` (or justified), and `supply == 1_000_000_000 × 10^decimals`. Pattern from [Neodyme](https://neodyme.io/en/blog/token-2022). |
| T-9 | **`transfer` instead of `transfer_checked` / wrong token program** | Using `token::transfer` or `anchor_spl::token::Transfer` against Token-2022: it fails for fee accounts (DoS) or, for extension-less legacy accounts (T-3), skips the fee. Passing legacy `Token` where Token-2022 is expected means the CPI goes to the wrong program. | High | Only `token_interface::transfer_checked` or `transfer_checked_with_fee`. The token program is `Interface<TokenInterface>` pinned by `address` to Token-2022 for this mint. Put `mint::token_program`, `token::token_program`, and `associated_token::token_program` on every account. Add a lint/grep in CI for `token::transfer(` and `Transfer {`. |
| T-10 | **Sandwiching the token→SOL conversion** | Buy flow sells harvested tokens on a DEX. A searcher front-runs (sells) and back-runs (buys), or pushes price with a bundle right before the crank. Predictable crank timing makes it easy. | High | `min_out` comes from program-side bounds (oracle or TWAP, or a max-slippage bps on a program-computed quote), never from the caller alone. Cap the size per crank and split over time. Pin the DEX program ID and pool address in config. Consider permissioned keeper plus private submission. Tax tokens being sold also pushes down the token's price; model this economically. |
| T-11 | **Front-running floor purchases** | Bots see the fee vault balance and the crank schedule, sweep floor NFTs first, and relist higher, so the program buys worse NFTs. | Medium | Less predictable schedule, spend caps, price caps (T-7). Accept some leakage; cap it with a per-NFT price cap. |
| T-12 | **Withheld fees stuck** | Fee vault or recipient account is frozen, or the withdraw destination is frozen, so `withdraw` fails. Or the program needs to close accounts that still have withheld fees. | Medium | No freeze authority (T-8). Harvest vault accounts before any close. Integration tests for close with withheld > 0. |
| T-13 | **Fee withheld inside our own vaults goes uncounted** | Every transfer *into* the escrow, fee vault, or prize vault leaves a withheld portion in that vault's own `TransferFeeAmount`. The vault's `amount` is lower than naive math expects, and the withheld part only comes back through harvest and withdraw. | Medium | Always use the post-transfer `amount` from a reloaded account. Include vault accounts in the harvest crank. |
| T-14 | **CPI Guard / MemoTransfer on user accounts** | A user account with CPI Guard enabled rejects our program's CPI transfer made with the owner's signature, and memo-required accounts reject incoming transfers with no memo ([Neodyme](https://neodyme.io/en/blog/token-2022)). Payouts fail, and batch payouts can be griefed. | Low–Medium | Prefer **pull** payouts (winner claims) over push. Where a user-side transfer is needed, have the user sign the top-level transfer or use an approved delegate. Never let one failing recipient block a batch. |

### 6.3 Lottery: tickets, snapshot, and the VRF draw

**Relevant VRF facts:**
- *Switchboard On-Demand* is commit then reveal. The tutorial's controls: check `seed_slot == clock.slot - 1` at commit; at commit, require `get_value(clock.slot)` to be `Err` (not yet revealed); store the randomness account key and `seed_slot`, and re-check both at settle; take all value at commit ("selective revelation attack" otherwise). `get_value` returns only when `clock.slot == reveal_slot`, so reveal and settle share a slot, usually one transaction ([tutorial](https://docs.switchboard.xyz/docs-by-chain/solana-svm/randomness/randomness-tutorial), [randomness.rs](https://github.com/switchboard-xyz/solana-sdk/blob/2121341/src/on_demand/accounts/randomness.rs)). Commit needs the randomness account's `authority` signature ([randomness_commit.rs](https://github.com/switchboard-xyz/solana-sdk/blob/2121341/src/on_demand/instructions/randomness_commit.rs)). Requests not revealed within about an hour are treated as expired ([Switchboard gitbook](https://github.com/switchboard-xyz/gitbook-randomness-on-demand)). The SDK's expected owner switches between mainnet `SBondMDrcV3K4kxZR1HNVT7osZxAHVHgYXL5Ze1oMUv` and devnet `Aio4gaXjXzJNVLtzwtNVmSqGKpANtXhybbkhtAC94ji2` depending on the build ([program_id.rs](https://github.com/switchboard-xyz/solana-sdk/blob/2121341/src/program_id.rs)).
- *ORAO VRF*: request account = PDA of the ORAO program derived from a 32-byte seed (`seeds::program = orao_solana_vrf::ID`). Fulfilment is asynchronous, so the program has to wait for `fulfilled_randomness()`. v0.4+ uses `RandomnessAccountData` ([ORAO README](https://github.com/orao-network/solana-vrf)). Program IDs: classic `VRFzZoJdhFWL8rkvu87LpKM3RbcVezpMEc6X5GVDr7y`, callback `VRFCBePmGTpZ234BhbzNNzmyg39Rgdd6VgdfhHwKypU`.

| ID | Threat | How an attacker does it | Severity if present | Required design control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L-1 | **Flash tickets (snapshot sniping)** | Bundle: buy tokens (or borrow), call `register` or trigger the snapshot, sell, all atomic ([Jito](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)). Or buy just before a known snapshot slot and sell right after. The transfer tax raises the cost but doesn't prevent it. | Critical (if tickets read live balances at one moment) | Don't derive tickets from a live `balance` read at one moment. Recommended: **stake/lock** tokens into a program vault. Tickets = `floor(staked / threshold)`, counted only if `stake_slot <= round.close_slot - MIN_HOLD_SLOTS`, and unstaking during an open round forfeits that round. The ticket set is frozen *before* randomness is committed. Alternatives (transfer-hook "last received" tracking, off-chain Merkle snapshot) add hook risk or trust (see Q3). |
| L-2 | **Double-counting the same tokens** | Register wallet A (tickets from A's balance), move tokens to B, register B, and repeat. | Critical | Tickets come only from tokens held by the program (staking vault), or from one atomic snapshot the attacker can't split. Never from "register now, read balance now" across many transactions. |
| L-3 | **Sybil splitting** | Spread holdings across many wallets to beat per-wallet caps or minimums. | Low with the brief's formula (remainders are discarded, so splitting can only lose tickets), High if any per-wallet bonus, floor, or square-root weighting is added | Keep tickets linear in stake with `floor`. No per-wallet minimum-ticket bonus and no per-wallet cap that splitting could get around. Charge ticket-record rent to the user. |
| L-4 | **Protocol-owned balances counted as tickets** | If tickets are ever balance-based: the hybrid escrow, LP/bonding-curve vaults, fee vault, and prize vault hold huge balances and "win", or whoever controls those accounts does. | High | With staking this can't happen by construction. With snapshots, use an explicit exclusion list in config, fixed at round open. |
| L-5 | **Attacker-supplied randomness account** | Pass an account with Switchboard's discriminator that the attacker created under another program, or an old Switchboard account whose revealed value is already known. `parse()` only checks the discriminator. | Critical | Check `randomness.owner == SWITCHBOARD_ON_DEMAND_ID` (mainnet ID pinned by build feature; CI asserts the mainnet build uses the mainnet ID). The **round** stores the randomness key at commit, and settle requires `address = round.randomness`. For ORAO, derive the request PDA from a seed the program generates (e.g. `hash(program_id, launch, round_id)`) with `seeds::program = ORAO_ID`. |
| L-6 | **Stale or reused randomness** | Reuse a randomness account from a previous round, or commit to one whose value was already revealed. For ORAO, pick a seed that was requested and fulfilled earlier. | Critical | Switchboard: at commit, `seed_slot == clock.slot - 1` and `get_value(clock.slot).is_err()`. Store `seed_slot`. At settle, require the same `seed_slot` and `get_value(clock.slot)`. ORAO: the program makes the request CPI itself in the same instruction that freezes tickets, with a fresh program-derived seed. The request fails if the PDA already exists. |
| L-7 | **Selective reveal / abort** | Whoever submits reveal plus settle simulates it first and doesn't send if the result is bad for them (e.g. the keeper holds tickets). If the program then allows a fresh commit after the expiry window, they get a re-roll. | High | Settle is **permissionless**, so anyone holding the revealed value can finish the draw. Ticket set and prize are frozen at commit. Re-commit after expiry is allowed only after a long timeout, is logged, and is rate-limited. Consider ORAO's oracle-pushed fulfilment so no requester-side reveal transaction is needed. Keeper and admin wallets are ineligible for tickets. Confirm whether the Switchboard reveal can be submitted by anyone or only the randomness authority (Q6). |
| L-8 | **Slot-hash / Clock / blockhash "randomness"** | Leaders and bots predict or influence it (Switchboard: "Block hashes are predictable… Timestamps are manipulable"). A bundle reverts unless it wins. | Critical | Forbid it in code. Grep in CI for `SlotHashes`, `Clock::get().*unix_timestamp` used as entropy, and `recent_blockhashes`. The only entropy source is the bound VRF value. (Note: MPL-Hybrid's reroll uses exactly this pattern; see H-4.) |
| L-9 | **Entries or stake changes after commit** | Add tickets or stake after randomness is committed but before reveal, when the value is still hidden from us but a TEE/oracle leak or the revealer could know it. | High | Round state machine `Open → Closed(snapshot) → Committed → Settled → Claimable → Finalized`. `stake`/`unstake`/`enter` only in `Open`. The snapshot total is written at `Closed`. Every instruction starts with `require!(round.state == X)`. |
| L-10 | **Winner selection bugs** | Modulo bias (`u8 % n`), off-by-one in cumulative ranges, iterating all tickets (runs out of compute, so the draw can be DoSed by spamming tiny stakes), several prizes from the same bytes. | Medium (bias), High (DoS or wrong winner) | `index = u128::from_le_bytes(rand[0..16]) % total_tickets` (bias is negligible at 128 bits). Store cumulative `[start, end)` per ticket record, and let the winner **prove** membership at claim instead of scanning. For prize *k*, use `hash(rand ‖ k)`. Define and test the duplicate-winner policy. |
| L-11 | **Keeper/admin manipulates round timing** | Admin closes the round early or late to include or exclude particular stakers, or cancels and restarts after seeing a bad draw. | High | Fixed `open_slot`/`close_slot` written at round creation and never editable. No `cancel` after `Committed`. Cancellation before commit refunds nothing (there's nothing to refund with staking) and is logged. |
| L-12 | **Ticket-record PDA confusion** | Records not bound to `(launch, round, owner)`. The attacker passes someone else's winning record, or a record from another round or launch. | Critical | Seeds `["ticket", launch, round_id.to_le_bytes(), owner]`. Claim requires `has_one = owner`, `owner: Signer`, and a `round` match. Records are created with `init` (never `init_if_needed`) per round. |

### 6.4 Prize vault and payouts (the distribution layer the brief flags)

| ID | Threat | How an attacker does it | Severity if present | Required design control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-1 | **Double claim** | Claim twice, or close the claim record and re-create it with `init` so the "claimed" flag resets (close/revival). | Critical | A `claimed: bool` on a record that is **never closed until the round is finalized**, or a separate `["claim", round, prize_idx]` PDA created with `init` (a second `init` fails). After Anchor `close`, the account is owned by System with reset data ([Anchor docs](https://docs.rs/anchor-lang/latest/anchor_lang/derive.Accounts.html)), so never treat "account missing" as "unclaimed". |
| P-2 | **Wrong winner paid** | `claim` checks only that the caller has *a* ticket record, not that the drawn index falls in its `[start, end)`. Or the destination account is supplied by the caller and not tied to the winner. | Critical | Claim checks `round.winning_index[k] ∈ [record.start, record.end)`, `record.owner == signer`, and that the destination is the winner's ATA via `associated_token::authority = winner`, `associated_token::mint`, `associated_token::token_program`. For Core NFTs, `new_owner = winner`. |
| P-3 | **Prize vault drained by a generic admin/emergency instruction** | An "emergency withdraw" or "rescue" instruction that sends anything anywhere is the classic distribution-layer backdoor. | Critical | No arbitrary-destination withdraw. If an unclaimed-prize path exists, it goes only to a fixed on-chain destination (the next round's prize vault), behind a timelock and a multisig. |
| P-4 | **NFT standard mismatch** | Prize NFTs bought from external collections may be Core, legacy Token Metadata, pNFTs (Token Auth Rules can block program-owned destinations or certain CPIs), or compressed (Bubblegum). A payout written for one fails for others, and prizes get stuck. | Medium–High | Decide Q9. Accept only the standards the program explicitly supports. Check at purchase time that the vault can later transfer the NFT (e.g. pNFT ruleset allows it). |
| P-5 | **Unbounded payout loops / push payouts** | Program pushes prizes to N winners in one instruction, and one frozen, closed, or CPI-guarded account blocks everyone. | Medium | Pull-based claims, one prize per instruction. Expiry plus rollover policy. |
| P-6 | **Token prizes silently shrink** | If prizes include taxed tokens, the winner receives `amount - fee`. | Low | Say so in the UI, or gross up with `calculate_inverse_epoch_fee`. |

### 6.5 Admin, upgrade, and authority custody

| ID | Threat | How an attacker does it | Severity if present | Required design control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-1 | **Upgrade authority compromise** | Single-key upgrade authority is stolen and a malicious program is deployed that signs for every PDA (escrow, fee vault, prize vault). | Critical | Upgrade authority in a multisig (e.g. Squads) with a timelock. Verified, reproducible builds (e.g. `solana-verify`; MPL-Hybrid itself added verified-build workflows in PRs #24, #25 and #27). A documented plan for freezing with `solana program set-upgrade-authority <ID> --final`, which is permanent ([Solana deploying docs](https://solana.com/docs/programs/deploying)). |
| A-2 | **Initialization front-running** | Attacker calls `initialize` or `create_config` before the team does and sets themselves as admin or fee recipient. | Critical | `initialize` requires `signer == program_data.upgrade_authority_address` (take `Program<'info, ThisProgram>` + `Account<'info, ProgramData>` with `constraint = program.programdata_address()? == Some(program_data.key())`), or a hardcoded admin pubkey. Global config seeds are fixed (`["config"]`) so only one can exist. |
| A-3 | **Over-powerful single admin** | One admin key can change fees, the buy allowlist, caps, round timing, the VRF queue, and the pause. | High | Separate roles: `admin` (multisig: config within hard-coded bounds), `keeper` (hot: crank only, no parameter changes), `pauser` (can pause new deposits or entries only, **never** withdraw). Two-step authority transfer (`propose` / `accept`). Events on every admin change, plus monitoring. |
| A-4 | **Parameter changes without bounds** | Admin sets `threshold = 1` or `u64::MAX`, the spend cap to infinity, or the VRF queue to one they control. | High | Hard-coded min/max in code for every parameter. VRF program ID and queue pinned as constants, not config. Parameters that affect an open round are snapshotted into the round at creation. |
| A-5 | **Mint and collection authority custody** | Token-2022 authorities (fee config, withdraw-withheld, metadata update) and the Core collection update authority are held by team hot wallets. | Critical | See T-1, T-2, H-2. Target state: mint authority `None`, freeze `None`, withdraw-withheld = program PDA, fee-config `None` or multisig, collection update authority = multisig (or a program PDA if our program must update metadata). Publish all of these. The brief already wants plain-language "what nobody can change" guarantees, and these are what should back them. |
| A-6 | **Anchor < 1.0 IDL takeover of zero-discriminator accounts** | The Anchor docs say that before 1.0, "program-owned accounts with zeroed discriminators … can be taken over via IDL instructions" ([constraints](https://www.anchor-lang.com/docs/references/account-constraints)). | Medium | Build with Anchor ≥ 1.0 (legacy IDL instructions replaced by Program Metadata, per the [1.0 notes](https://www.anchor-lang.com/docs/updates/release-notes/1-0-0)), or never leave program-owned accounts zeroed. |
| A-7 | **Devnet/mainnet constant mix-ups** | A mainnet build still points at devnet Switchboard IDs, a test marketplace, or a test mint. | High | Constants chosen by Cargo feature, plus a CI test that asserts the mainnet feature's constants. The brief says testnets and throwaway keys only until the audit. |

### 6.6 Cross-cutting Anchor account-validation threats (apply to all programs)

Reference patterns: [coral-xyz/sealevel-attacks](https://github.com/coral-xyz/sealevel-attacks), [Anchor account constraints](https://www.anchor-lang.com/docs/references/account-constraints), [Anchor `Accounts` docs](https://docs.rs/anchor-lang/latest/anchor_lang/derive.Accounts.html).

| ID | Threat | How an attacker does it | Severity if present | Required design control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| X-1 | **`UncheckedAccount`/`AccountInfo` with no checks** | Pass any account where a vault, mint, config, or program is expected (type cosplay, owner spoofing). | Critical | Use `Account<T>`, `InterfaceAccount<T>`, `Program<T>`, `Signer`. Every `UncheckedAccount` gets a `/// CHECK:` comment **and** an `address =`, `seeds =`, or `owner =` constraint, and the audit lists each one. Anchor 1.0 warns on raw `AccountInfo` in `Accounts` ([1.0 notes](https://www.anchor-lang.com/docs/updates/release-notes/1-0-0)). |
| X-2 | **`Interface<TokenInterface>` accepts either token program** | Attacker passes legacy SPL Token and an SPL mint where the Token-2022 mint was expected, or mixes programs between mint and accounts. | High | Pin the token program with `address = spl_token_2022::ID` for the taxed mint, and set `mint::token_program`, `token::token_program`, `associated_token::token_program` on every related account ([docs](https://docs.rs/anchor-lang/latest/anchor_lang/derive.Accounts.html)). |
| X-3 | **Missing `has_one` / relationship checks** | Pass a config, round, or vault belonging to another launch (factory setting). | Critical | `has_one = mint`, `has_one = collection`, `has_one = authority` on configs. Every child PDA seeds include the `launch` key. |
| X-4 | **Non-canonical bumps / PDA sharing** | User-supplied bump, or one PDA reused across roles (e.g. fee authority is also the prize vault authority), so one bug exposes everything. | High | `bump` without a value at init and stored `bump = x.bump` afterwards. A distinct seed prefix per role: `fee_authority`, `fee_vault`, `prize_vault`, `escrow`, `stake_vault`. |
| X-5 | **`init_if_needed` reinitialisation** | Call an instruction that re-runs initialisation on an existing account to reset owner, claimed flags, or stake timestamps. Anchor warns explicitly about this ([docs](https://docs.rs/anchor-lang/latest/anchor_lang/derive.Accounts.html)). | High | Separate `init` instructions. `init_if_needed` only for user ATAs (with full `associated_token::*` constraints), never for state accounts. |
| X-6 | **Duplicate mutable accounts** | Pass the same account as `from` and `to`, or as two different users' records, to double-count. | High | Anchor ≥ 1.0 rejects duplicate mutable `Account`/`LazyAccount`/`InterfaceAccount`/`Migration` by default, but **not** `UncheckedAccount`, `Signer`, `SystemAccount`, `AccountLoader` ([constraints](https://www.anchor-lang.com/docs/references/account-constraints)). Add explicit `key() != key()` constraints wherever those types are involved. Never use `dup` on value-bearing accounts. |
| X-7 | **Close / revival** | Close a record, then refund lamports in the same transaction or re-`init` it later to replay a claim or reset state. | High | Don't close claim, stake, or ticket records until the round is `Finalized`. Round IDs are part of the seeds, so old records can't be reused. Never infer "never existed" from "empty". |
| X-8 | **`remaining_accounts` trusted blindly** | Extra vaults, ticket records, or harvest sources passed with no owner, key, or dedupe checks. | Critical (if used for value) | Validate each one (owner program, PDA derivation, mint, no duplicates). Cap the count. Avoid `remaining_accounts` for anything that moves value. |
| X-9 | **Subtractive `realloc` on a vault** | Anchor docs warn that a subtractive realloc refunds *all* lamports above the new rent minimum to `realloc::payer`. | Medium | Never realloc accounts that hold lamports as value. |
| X-10 | **Arithmetic** | Overflow or underflow in ticket ranges, fee math, `max - min`, casts. | High | `checked_*` everywhere, `u128` intermediates, `overflow-checks = true` in the release profile, and property tests. |
| X-11 | **CPI result ignored / post-state unchecked** | Treating a CPI as successful without checking its effect (compare MPL-Hybrid's `_transfer_nft_result`). | Medium | Always use `?` on CPIs, then `reload()` and assert the expected balances and owners. |
| X-12 | **Signer privilege passed through to untrusted programs** | Forwarding the user's signature, or a PDA signature, into a CPI to a caller-chosen program. | Critical | Only CPI into pinned programs (T-6). Use `invoke_signed` with PDA seeds only for the exact expected instruction. |

## 7. "Must-have before audit" design checklist

**Architecture and authorities**
- [ ] Q1 is answered in writing: the hybrid token is a legacy SPL mint (MPL-Hybrid as-is), or a Token-2022-aware swap program is written or forked (H-1).
- [ ] Mint is created and configured atomically by our launch instruction: 1B minted, `mint_authority = None`, `freeze_authority = None` (or a written justification), `supply` checked (T-3, T-8, brief).
- [ ] Extension allowlist is enforced on-chain at launch, and PermanentDelegate, TransferHook, MintCloseAuthority, DefaultAccountState, NonTransferable, ConfidentialTransfer(+Fee), and Pausable are rejected unless explicitly justified (T-8).
- [ ] `withdraw_withheld_authority` = program PDA, checked on-chain before launch activates (T-1).
- [ ] `transfer_fee_config_authority` is `None` or multisig. Max bps and `maximum_fee` are documented. The program reads the current-epoch fee on every use (T-2).
- [ ] Core collection update authority (= MPL-Hybrid escrow/recipe authority) sits in a multisig. There is no hot co-signer for swaps (H-2, H-3).
- [ ] Upgrade authority is a multisig with a timelock. Verified builds are set up. A freeze plan is written (A-1).
- [ ] `initialize` is gated by the upgrade authority or a hardcoded admin. Roles are separated (admin / keeper / pauser). Authority transfer is two-step (A-2, A-3).
- [ ] Every admin parameter has hard-coded bounds. VRF, DEX, marketplace, and MPL Core program IDs are constants selected by build feature (A-4, A-7).

**Token handling**
- [ ] No `token::transfer`/`Transfer {}` anywhere. Only `transfer_checked`/`transfer_checked_with_fee` (T-9, with a CI grep).
- [ ] The token program is pinned to Token-2022 for the taxed mint. `*::token_program` constraints are on every mint, token account, and ATA (X-2).
- [ ] All amounts are fee-aware: amounts received are measured from reloaded balances, or computed with `calculate_epoch_fee` and enforced with `transfer_checked_with_fee` (H-6, T-13).
- [ ] Harvest and withdraw accounting uses before/after deltas of the mint's `withheld_amount` and the vault balance (T-5).
- [ ] The escrow backing invariant is asserted after every swap. The ratio divides 1B base units cleanly (H-10, brief).

**Swap**
- [ ] Reroll is disabled, or it's VRF-based. No SlotHashes/Clock entropy (H-4, L-8).
- [ ] If escrowed NFTs differ in value, the program picks the outgoing asset. Fee-bought NFTs never enter the hybrid escrow (H-5).
- [ ] Asset checks: owner program = MPL Core, collection = config, current owner = escrow or signer (H-7).

**Buy flow**
- [ ] No arbitrary CPI. Marketplace and DEX program IDs are pinned. The vault PDA signs only pinned CPIs (T-6).
- [ ] Collection allowlist, per-NFT price cap, and per-epoch spend cap are enforced on-chain. Post-condition: NFT owned by the prize vault PDA (T-7).
- [ ] Token→SOL swap has program-bounded `min_out`, a size cap per crank, and pinned pool (T-10).

**Lottery**
- [ ] Ticket model chosen (Q3). Recommended: stake/lock vault with `floor(staked / threshold)` and a minimum holding period in slots. No live-balance reads (L-1, L-2).
- [ ] Round state machine with a `require!` on state in every instruction. Ticket set frozen before commit. `open_slot`/`close_slot` immutable (L-9, L-11).
- [ ] Randomness account owner is checked against the pinned VRF program. Key and `seed_slot` (or ORAO seed) are stored at commit and required at settle. Freshness and not-yet-revealed checks are in place (L-5, L-6).
- [ ] Settle is permissionless. Keeper and admin are ineligible to win. Expiry and re-commit policy is written and rate-limited (L-7).
- [ ] Winner index uses 128-bit modulo. Cumulative ranges with claim-time proof. No full scans (L-10).
- [ ] Ticket, claim, and stake PDAs are seeded by `(launch, round, owner/prize)`. `init` only. Nothing is closed before `Finalized` (L-12, P-1, X-7).
- [ ] Claims are pull-based. The destination is tied to the winner. There is no arbitrary-destination withdraw from the prize vault (P-2, P-3, P-5).

**Anchor hygiene**
- [ ] Anchor ≥ 1.0. Every `UncheckedAccount` is documented and constrained. No `init_if_needed` on state. Explicit key-inequality checks for non-serialising duplicate types. `remaining_accounts` fully validated. Checked math with `overflow-checks = true` (X-1…X-12).
- [ ] Every CPI result is checked with `?`, followed by `reload()` and post-state assertions (X-11).

**Testing and ops**
- [ ] Negative tests for every row in §6 marked Critical or High (wrong program ID, wrong mint, wrong owner, duplicate accounts, stale randomness, double claim, flash-stake bundle, fee changes across the epoch boundary).
- [ ] Property/fuzz tests on fee math, ticket ranges, and backing invariants (e.g. Trident or proptest).
- [ ] Events on every state transition and admin action. A monitoring and alerting plan for authority changes, `update_escrow`/`update_recipe`, `SetTransferFee`, and large vault outflows.
- [ ] Devnet/localnet only, with throwaway keys, until the third-party audit is done (brief).

## 8. Open design questions (answers change the risk)

Ranked by how much the answer changes the risk.

1. **Q1: Is the hybrid (MPL-Hybrid) token the same mint as the Token-2022 taxed token, or are "hybrid launches" and "taxed launches" separate launch types?** MPL-Hybrid supports only legacy SPL Token today (FAQ plus source). If they're the same mint, we need a custom or forked swap program (H-1, H-6).
2. **Q2: Who holds `withdraw_withheld_authority` and `transfer_fee_config_authority`, and what are the fee bps and `maximum_fee`?** A program PDA plus `None`/multisig is low risk. An EOA is Critical (T-1, T-2).
3. **Q3: How are tickets counted and how is the "minimum holding period" enforced?** Options: stake/lock vault (recommended), transfer-hook balance tracking, or an off-chain Merkle snapshot. When is the snapshot taken relative to the VRF commit, and which protocol-owned accounts are excluded (L-1, L-2, L-4)?
4. **Q4: Is the program upgradeable, by whom (single key / multisig / timelock), and is there a date or condition for freezing it?** Also: who holds the Core collection update authority (A-1, H-2)?
5. **Q5: Do fee-funded purchases touch external marketplaces and DEXes on-chain?** If so, which marketplaces (Tensor, Magic Eden, others), which DEX or pool for token→SOL, who chooses the listing, and how is "floor price" established? Or does a multisig operator buy off-chain and deposit into the prize vault (T-6, T-7, T-10)?
6. **Q6: Which VRF provider (Switchboard On-Demand or ORAO), who runs the draw crank, can the reveal be submitted by anyone, and what happens if a reveal expires (about 1 hour for Switchboard)?** (L-5 to L-7)
7. **Q7: How does "unwrap back into the exact tokens" hold under a transfer tax?** Token-2022's TransferFeeConfig has no per-account exemption. Is the swap grossed up, or is the tax meant to apply to swaps (H-6)?
8. **Q8: Is MPL-Hybrid metadata reroll wanted, and do NFTs in the escrow differ in value (rarity)?** This decides H-4 and H-5.
9. **Q9: Which NFT standards can prize NFTs be: Core, Token Metadata, pNFT (auth rules), compressed?** (P-4)
10. **Q10: Is this a factory (many launches per program) or one program per launch?** A factory needs launch-scoped seeds and `has_one` everywhere (X-3, T-4).
11. **Q11: Is there a pause, who holds it, and what exactly does it stop?** Pausing must never enable withdrawals (A-3).
12. **Q12: Policy for unclaimed prizes (expiry, rollover destination) and for duplicate winners across multiple prizes in one round?** (P-3, L-10)
13. **Q13: Mint decimals and the exact swap ratio**, so that `1_000_000_000 × 10^decimals` divides by the ratio and fee rounding is understood.
14. **Q14: Does the bonding curve / graduation (mentioned as a reusable idea in the brief) get built?** If so, it needs its own threat model: its vaults interact with the fee, ticket, and exclusion logic.
15. **Q15: Is any backend co-signer planned for any instruction** (allowlist, KYC, swap co-sign)? Every co-signer key is a hot key with the powers of whatever it signs.
